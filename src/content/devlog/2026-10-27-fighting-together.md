## Scavs for the whole squad

The host's game runs the scavs for everyone, and brings two extra for each friend who came. Each scav goes after whichever of you it can see, switches when it loses you, and turns on whoever shoots it. Hits count where they're seen, so if your round hits a scav on your screen, it hits. Friendly fire is on.

## Shared loot

Everyone has the same crates, bodies and things on the ground. What one of you takes is gone for the others, and what you put in is there for them. One person searches a thing at a time; anyone else is told who's in it. Drops land on everyone's game where they came to rest on yours.

## When it goes wrong

If you're killed, you lie where you fell with everything you had, and your squad can search your body for it. Once your raid is over, the summary offers **Watch squadmates**: a camera over a friend's shoulder, looking where they look, with click or Space for the next. If the host goes back to the hideout while friends are still out there, they're asked first, and anyone still in the raid keeps what they have.

## Polish

The last round checks that everyone is on the same build before they can join, holds on to anything that arrives while a slower PC is still loading the map, keeps the extra scavs away from the squad's start, and lets squadmates lean round corners as far as you can, head showing.
