## Rain that fills the street

The rain used to be drops falling round you and not much else, so ten metres off it was a dry day again. Now a downpour fills the whole view, right up to your face. The drops near you are thin, each a different thickness, length and brightness, and the ones right by your face are out of focus. Like little glass rods they bend whatever is behind them, a touch darker at the edges and paler down the middle, so you can pick them out one by one instead of seeing a white curtain. Further off the rain is long, thin, see-through streaks at five distances out to about 50 metres, finer the further they are, so it thickens into the grey haze down the street. It's only ever in the open air between you and what you're looking at, so it never falls through a wall, and it's lit by the sky: against the clouds it shows a little darker, and against dark houses, trees and windows it shows pale. Every drop, near or far, leans the same way in the wind, sways when it gusts and slides past as you walk. Inside the old Sainsbury's it's gone, and from an open house you see it through the windows.

## Wet streets

In the rain the tarmac goes dark and shiny, and while it pours, water lies in the hollows between its stones. It runs off to the kerbs and stands along the gutters, wider where they dip between the blocked drains, and the potholes fill to the brim. The puddles in the dips of the road have a dark soaked edge and mirror the sky and the houses, with rings where the drops land close up. Nothing on them sparkles or crawls as you move. The kerbs and speed humps stay clear of standing water. The cars are wet too: darker, glossy, with beads of water on the roofs and bonnets.
