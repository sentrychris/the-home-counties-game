## Thinner, fainter rain

The first heavy rain was too much: it took up most of the screen. It turns out the far-off rain, the long streaks out to about 50 metres, was what filled the view, not the drops falling round you. It's now very faint, a touch of pale streaks against the houses and trees that thickens the haze down the street, and the drops near you are fewer, so you can pick them out one by one. Rain also only ever adds light now. Nothing in it darkens what's behind it, so it can't draw a dark speck or line that flickers as you move. The little splashes where drops land on railings, signs and bins are pale spray now. Before, they came out nearly black.

## Rain on your sight

Out in the open, rain gathers on your sight the way it does on a camera lens. Small drops land, sit for a while and dry off, and now and then a big one runs down in jerks, leaving a thin wet trail and a few beads behind it. Each drop is a little lens, so it shows the street round it turned upside down, with a darker rim and a glint where the light from above catches it. It gathers faster when you look up into the rain, and dries off a few seconds after you get under a roof or go indoors. If you'd rather not have it, untick "Rain on screen" in the camera effects in Settings.
