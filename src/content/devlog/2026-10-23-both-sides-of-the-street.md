The map data gives every house the right footprint, but not the right look. So both sides of Duncombe Street were rebuilt house by house from photos of the real street.

## The east side

From the Osborne Street corner down to Water Eaton Road: narrow two-storey terraces in cream, white, brown and mustard pebbledash, glazed porches and tiled porch hoods, a garage yard, semis with bay windows, and a red-brick terrace at the south end. The Victorian corner house across Osborne Street has its front gable and castellated bay.

## The west side

The railway side has a bungalow, a red-brick terrace with arched passages between the houses, the Milton Keynes Jamee Masjid, and one long terrace of two-storey bay windows, each house painted differently.

## Lived in, then left

A fifth realism pass added what the street looks like after a few years with nobody in charge: moving clouds and sun rays, rubbish on the pavements, fly-tipping on the spare ground, graffiti on the hoardings, a burnt-out van, and roadblocks of barriers and sandbags at the ends of the street.

A depth pass followed. Brick and roof tiles have real depth now, walls have proper copings, windows have rooms behind them, light bounces off the walls, and there's smoke, dust and falling leaves. After the first play-test the sun was softened, and distant gunfire and explosions were added to the background, so it never feels quite safe.
