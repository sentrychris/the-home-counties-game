## What a round hits

Until now a round hit everything the same way: a round hole, a grey puff and a few dark squares. Now what you see and hear depends on what it hit.

- **Steel.** Lamp posts, bollards, the railway's rails and masts: a white-hot flash, then a shower of sparks, short streaks of hot metal that cool from white through yellow and orange to a dull red as they fall, and bounce on the ground below. The round leaves a bright splash of lead on the paint, and heavy steel clangs.
- **Thinner metal.** Cars, corrugated sheets, sign posts and railings ring, throw about half as many sparks, and the round punches a hole with the paint flaked off round it to bare metal.
- **Brick, concrete and stone.** A crack, a puff of dust the colour of what was hit (grey, or brick red), and chips that tumble off, bounce and lie on the ground for a moment. The round breaks a ragged crater out of the face, a real hollow that the light falls into, fresh and paler inside round a dark pit. Now and then there are a few sparks too, as the AK's rounds have steel cores.
- **Wood.** Fences, doors, crates and pallets: a dull knock, splinters and pale wood dust, and a hole with the fibres torn out along the grain.
- **Earth and grass.** A thump and a spurt of dirt thrown up, with clods falling back. Bare earth and gravel keep a dark crater.
- **Glass, plastic, hedges and soft things.** A smaller hole and a little dust, or a puff of leaves.

The sparks, chips, splinters and clods all bounce off whatever is under them, the ground or a car's roof, and come to rest there.

## How it sounds

The hits are recordings from the same pack as the AK's shots and the cracks of rounds going past: rounds hitting concrete, sheet metal, heavy steel, wood and earth. Brick, stone, glass and plastic use the nearest of those, pitched a little higher or lower. Close by in the open you hear them as they were recorded. Each one now also comes in three more versions, eight takes of each, so a hit sounds like where you hear it from:

- **In a room** (the houses, the pub): with a short, tight echo off the walls.
- **In the old Sainsbury's:** with a longer echo off the big hall's hard walls and floor.
- **Far off** (over 30 metres away out in the open): duller, with the street's echo coming back off the houses, and a moment late, as sound takes its time to reach you.

## The flash

A round striking steel lights up what's round it for a moment. In bright sunshine you hardly notice it, as with a real one, but at night or indoors it lights the wall, the car or the pavement round it for a twentieth of a second.

## How it's done

The sparks are drawn as streaks: each one is stretched along the way it's flying by how fast it's going, as a camera with its shutter open for a moment would see it, and is never drawn thinner than a pixel and a bit, so they don't flicker in and out far off. The chips and splinters are little broken flakes that tumble as they fly, each a shade lighter or darker than the next, coloured as what they came off. The marks are pictures drawn for the game; a crater also carries its shape, so the side of the hollow facing the light is lit and the other side is in shade, and it looks dug out of the wall rather than painted on. The dust takes the colour of what was hit.
