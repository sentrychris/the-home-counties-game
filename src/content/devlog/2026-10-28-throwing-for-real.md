## The throw

Throwing is now a whole motion instead of a grenade leaving the screen. The grenade comes up as the rifle drops; hold the button and your left hand comes over, hooks the ring and yanks the pin out; your arm draws back by your shoulder while your other hand points the way; let go and it whips over past your ear, your body turning into it, and follows through as the rifle comes back up. Each move eases through its poses with a little give, and the view leans with the throw.

## Flight and bounce

In the air a grenade feels only gravity and air drag, tumbling as it was spun off the fingers. What it lands on decides the rest. Concrete, wood and metal give a lively bounce and a roll of a few metres. Gravel, grass and hedges soak it up and stop it within a metre. The cans of the flashbang and smoke roll round in an arc and stop sooner than the M67's ball. Scavs allow for all of this when they lob one at you.

## Distance and cooking

An average soldier throws an M67 about 35 metres at best, and so do you now: about 26 metres looking level and 37 looking 20 degrees up. Underhand is for a few metres. With a frag's pin out and the button held, **R** lets the spoon fly and the fuse starts burning in your hand, so it goes off sooner after it lands, or in the air. There's no timer on the screen. Count it yourself, and don't hold on too long.

The hand also got a proper grip: fuse up, thumb along the spoon, fingers round the body, fitted so nothing cuts into the grenade.
