## Real track

The railway along the west of the map was a flat strip that looked more like a toy than a railway. It's now four tracks of the West Coast Main Line built the way the real thing is: a raised bed of grey ballast with sloping shoulders, steel rails of the real profile on concrete sleepers held down with Pandrol clips, rust on the rail sides and polished steel on top, and weeds pushing up through the stones.

## The crossing and the wires

Where Water Eaton Road crosses, there's a half-barrier level crossing: one barrier down, one snapped off, the lights board, stop lines and warning signs back up the road. Overhead line portals march along the line carrying their wires, with one span cut down and hanging. The road deck across the crossing was redone a build later so it sits flush with the rail tops, and some flat green patches in the ballast were grown over.

All of it costs well under a millisecond a frame.
