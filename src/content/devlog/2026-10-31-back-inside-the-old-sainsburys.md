## Made real

The inside of the old Sainsbury's was the plainest place on the map: flat floors, plain boxes for shelves, and a ceiling like a sheet of card. It's been rebuilt from photos of real abandoned supermarkets.

The floor is terrazzo, dusty and water-stained. The suspended ceiling has tiles missing and hanging down, with daylight coming through the gaps in shafts. The shelving is proper supermarket shelving, some of it pushed over. At the back, whoever camped here left mattresses, sleeping bags and a tarp.

## Something left on the shelves

The shelves aren't bare any more. There are 64 products, all British in look: made-up own brands and value ranges, traffic-light labels, prices in pence, barcodes and shelf-edge tickets. Most of what's left is what nobody wanted: tea, dishwasher tablets, bleach, washing powder. A plastic strip curtain hangs in the warehouse doorway and a steel railing runs by the entrance.

All of that added a little to the frame time, so the shelves were then grouped into bigger batches and drawn only when you're close. It now costs the same as the empty store did.

## Gloomier

The first version was too bright: the white ceiling and shelves glowed, the rifle in your hands shone, and on a cloudy day the inside was lit as if the roof wasn't there. Now the store keeps the sun out whatever the weather, the ceiling is the darkest thing in it, there's a faint haze in the light from the roof, and only a faint light shows the rifle in your hands. It's dark in there, and that's the point.
