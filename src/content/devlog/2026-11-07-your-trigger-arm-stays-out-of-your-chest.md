## Your arm was in your chest

Crouch, lean and aim, then look at yourself in photo mode, and your trigger arm went through your body. The animations put the right elbow where they liked, then the lean and the aim pushed the hands to where the rifle has to be. Kneeling, that left the elbow 2 to 8 cm from the middle of your chest, so the forearm went through your chest and the wrist bent up to 75 degrees. The elbows could also bend sideways, which a real elbow can't do.

## Now it's set like a real arm

Once your hands are on the rifle, each arm is now set the way a person holds one:

- Your right elbow sits down and out to the side, a little higher when your cheek is on the stock.
- Your left elbow tucks down under the handguard.
- Both elbows bend like hinges, as real ones do.
- Each forearm takes half of the wrist's turn, so the wrist doesn't twist round on itself.
- Each hand can turn a little round what it holds, so the wrist stays as straight as it can. The right turns round the pistol grip and the left round the handguard.

The rifle doesn't move: it stays exactly where you aim it.

In every pose we check, standing, crouched, leaning either way, looking up and down, the right elbow is now 20 to 31 cm out from the middle of your chest and the wrist bends no more than 40 degrees. Squadmates see you the same way in co-op.

The pictures are from Duncombe Street, kneeling behind a wrecked car and leaning out past it.
