## Rails

The AK has a new **Handguard** slot on the gunsmith bench. The wooden lower handguard it comes with has nowhere to hang anything. Swap it for the alloy **railed handguard** and it opens a **Left rail** and an **Under rail**.

The Hi-Power has no rail of its own, so a light needs the **rail adapter** first, which clamps round the front of the trigger guard.

## Two lights

- **Scout weapon light.** About a thousand lumens, a tight beam that reaches around 55 metres. AK only, on either rail.
- **Compact weapon light.** A wider, even beam for rooms and stairs, out to about 40 metres. Under the AK's handguard, or on the Hi-Power.

Press <kbd>T</kbd> to turn it on or off, with a proper click. <kbd>Alt</kbd>+<kbd>T</kbd> still checks your ammo, and switching ammo type has moved to <kbd>Y</kbd>.

## What it looks like

A bright hot spot in the middle, with a soft spill round it that fades away, the way a real torch looks on a wall. It throws real shadows, and glossy things shine in it. In clear air you can't see the beam itself; in mist and smoke there's a faint cone.

The first version lit up the front of your own rifle and turned gun smoke into a white puff. Both are fixed: your light no longer shines back on your gun, and smoke glows softly instead.

## Being seen

A light works both ways. Scavs spot you much sooner when you're shining one their way, up to two and a half times as fast, and at night they see you from as far away as they would by day. In a squad, everyone sees your beam, and looking straight into a squadmate's light dazzles.

## Where to get them

Carl sells railed handguards, rail adapters and compact lights from the start, and scout lights once he knows you. Now and then a scav turns up with a railed handguard on its AK.
