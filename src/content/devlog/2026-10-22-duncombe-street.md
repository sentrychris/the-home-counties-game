The grey box proved the loop. The next step was a real place: Duncombe Street in Bletchley, Milton Keynes, a long row of terraces between the railway and the old Sainsbury's.

## Built from map data

The street isn't drawn by hand. Roads and building outlines come from open map data (Overture Maps, which includes OpenStreetMap), and a script turns them into the level: 201 houses, pavements, gardens, crates and scav spawns, with exits at the Station footpath and Osborne Street. The first version was flat colours, but every house stood where it does in real life.

## Art passes

Photo textures and an overcast sky went on first, then models: the rifle, cars, trees, bins and street furniture. The scavs became animated characters carrying the same AK as you.

## Realism passes

Four realism passes followed through the night, each checked against Street View photos of the street:

1. Proper windows, road detail, and wear and tear.
2. Grass you can see in 3D, hedges, trees and a horizon.
3. Telegraph poles and their wires, fences, clutter and weathering.
4. The details that make it Duncombe Street: white enamel name plates low on the front walls, pebbledash and render in white, cream and mustard, tall chimneys with TV aerials, double yellow lines down one side, and tactile paving at the corners.

The retirement flats on the corner of Osborne Street and Clifford Avenue went in next, with a ground-floor lounge you can walk into. A lighting pass finished the night: a lower sun, real shadows, and lit rooms behind the windows.
