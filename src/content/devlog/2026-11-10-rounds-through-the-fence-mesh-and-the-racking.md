## Seen through, but solid

The fence panels round the Clifford Avenue housing site and the site by the north wall are wire mesh: you can see straight through them. The pallet racking in the old Sainsbury's warehouse is mostly air, steel uprights and beams with gaps between the shelves. But to a round, each fence panel was a solid sheet, and each run of racking a solid block from the floor to the top shelf. You could see a scav through them and not hit it.

## Now

Rounds go through whatever you can see through. A fence panel's mesh lets them by, and its top and bottom rails and end posts, steel tube about 45 mm across, stop them. In the warehouse a round goes through the gaps between the shelves, and the uprights, the beams, the pallets and the wrapped loads and boxes on them stop it. The thin bracing doesn't.

It works both ways. Scavs see you through the mesh and the gaps and shoot at you through them, and they no longer count a fence panel or a run of racking as cover. Where a board or a banner is tied over a fence panel (the plywood and painted boards on Clifford Avenue, the banners at the north site), it still hides you, and for now it stops rounds where it covers the mesh.

Nothing else has changed. You, the scavs, thrown grenades and dropped things still bump into the fences and the racking, and the scavs walk round them as before. Your rifle's barrel still stops against a fence panel's mesh.

The pictures are cloud renders of a piece of the map. Each orange line is a round's path from the shooter to where it stopped.
