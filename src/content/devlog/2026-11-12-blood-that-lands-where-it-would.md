## Where the blood goes

Until now a round into a scav threw up a red puff and that was all. Now it leaves blood where blood would go, and only as much as a real wound would:

- **The way in.** A quick puff of fine blood back out the way the round came, gone in a moment.
- **The way out.** A round that goes right through throws more out of the far side, a fast cone of mist and droplets. A full metal jacket goes through the chest about three times in four, armour-piercing more often, a hollow point about one in three, the pistol's rounds less often, a knife never.
- **On a wall or a car** within a couple of metres behind him: a burst of droplets crowding into a patch about a hand across where the round's line meets it, thinning out to fine drops further round. Struck at a slant, the drops stretch out with their tails pointing the way they flew. Now and then a heavy splash runs down a wall over a few seconds.
- **On a window**, the spray is on the glass, cut to the pane, and goes with the glass when it breaks. Struck on the frame, it's on the frame and the panes round it.
- **On the ground**, with nothing close behind him, the heavier drops land a metre or two on.

A helmet or plate that stops the round stops the blood too, and you get a puff of the carrier's dust instead.

## The hurt and the dead

A scav you've hurt drips as he goes, a few drops every step or two for up to a minute or so (less for a graze to the head), so a sharp eye can follow him round a corner. A dead one starts to pool under the wound a second or two after he comes to rest, spreading over a minute or two to about 70 cm to a metre across for one wound, out from under him, and about a metre and a half for several. Shoot a body again and it bleeds only a little: the heart has stopped.

Everything dries as the raid goes on, from fresh red to a darker brown over five minutes, pools over fifteen, so you can tell an old fight from one that's just happened.

## How it's done

The drops, the drips and the runs down a wall are cut from public domain photographs of real blood (Texture Ninja); the sprays and pools are drawn by a script from forensic reference, and the drops, runs and pools have a wet shine and a little depth. On a wall, car or the ground they're projected decals; on glass they're cards fitted to the pane, since decals can't draw on see-through glass. Every spray lands after its flight time, falling a little on the way.

It's cheap: no new pass over the screen, at most 90 stains and 14 pools at once (the oldest go first), each fading out past 32 m. If you'd rather not see it, **Settings > Picture > Camera effects > Blood splatter** turns it off (the puffs stay).
