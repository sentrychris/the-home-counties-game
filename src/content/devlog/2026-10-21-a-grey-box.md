Every extraction shooter needs the same loop before it needs anything pretty: go in, take what you can, and get out alive. So The Home Counties started in Godot 4 as a grey box: a flat yard of coloured blocks, a rifle, some crates and a green extraction zone.

## The loop

By the end of the first evening the whole loop was there:

- **Walk, sprint, crouch and jump**, with a rifle that holds 30 rounds.
- **Crates to search**, and a raid timer counting down.
- **A stash** between raids. You tick what to take in, and whatever you bring out is added to it. The game saves as you go.
- **Death costs you.** Die or run out of time and everything you were carrying is gone.
- **Scavs**, which at this point were capsules with rifles.

## Aiming, bleeding and a backpack

The next pass made it feel like the genre: aiming down the sights, weapon sway, and a little weight to how you start and stop. The list of items became a grid inventory with a backpack, carry weight and items you can turn round to fit. Health was split into body parts, so a leg hit slows you down and a bleed keeps hurting until you deal with it. Ammo came in types, full metal jacket and armour-piercing.

The scavs got smarter the same night. They take cover, hold angles, try to flank you and shout to each other: "Changing mag!", "Cover me, reloading!"

> The grey box is still in the game as the **Practice yard**, where new things get tested.
