## Frames on a bare wall

At the checkpoint end of Osborne Street, the two gabled houses (one rendered, one in white-painted brick) had window frames upstairs with nothing in them. You could see the wall straight through the frame, as if someone had screwed an empty frame to the brick.

Every window in the game sits in a real opening in its wall: the frame set back in the opening, the glass behind it, and behind the glass a room, drawn by the glass itself. The upstairs window on these houses is on the front of a two-storey bay, and the bay's wall wasn't one the house cut its openings into. So the window was stood on the face of the wall instead, lifted out just far enough for its frame to show. But the glass sits a few centimetres behind the front of the frame, and that left it a centimetre inside the wall, out of sight.

## Now

The window upstairs on each house is now cut into the front of its bay, like every other window on the street. It has its glass, a room behind it, and the net curtains, curtains or blind it was always meant to have. The gabled pair at the Oliver Road end of the same row had the same fault and has the same fix (one of their windows is boarded up, as it was). One more window had it too: the little one high in the front gable of a rendered house on Duncombe Street, near the Water Eaton Road end. There's no wall there to cut into, so it now stands out from the gable just far enough to show its glass.

Nothing else on the map has moved. The pictures are cloud renders of that end of Osborne Street in the afternoon sun.
