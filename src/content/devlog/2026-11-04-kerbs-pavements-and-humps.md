## Built like a real street

The pavements were neat grey slabs, every kerb was one long strip of concrete, and the humps on Duncombe Street were three flat layers stacked on the road. None of it looked like a street that has been dug up and patched for a hundred years.

## Pavements

The pavements are tarmac now, as on the real street, and the years of work show in them: trenches out to the houses where the water and gas were put in, long ones down the street, odd squares where someone fixed something. Older patches have gone grey and newer ones are still dark, each edged with black bitumen. The covers are where you'd expect them: stopcocks and meter lids by the front doors, phone line boxes by the telegraph poles, iron inspection covers, gas valves and fire hydrant plates.

## Kerbs

Every kerb is laid in kerbstones, each with a joint and a shade of its own. A few are stained or newer than the rest, and the odd one has sunk or lifted. Where you cross a side road they drop flush, with buff blister paving behind them. At drives, paved-over front gardens and the ways into the car parks they drop low, and the pavement dips down to meet them.

## Humps

Duncombe Street's three humps are round-topped tarmac now, 75 mm high. They were laid over the old road in a darker tarmac and sealed round the edges, with worn white triangles up the ramps.
