## Room by room

The eight houses you can go into on Duncombe Street are no longer empty shells. Each one is laid out like a typical British terrace: a front room with a fire surround, a dining room through a pair of doors, a narrow galley kitchen, a bathroom, and two bedrooms upstairs.

Everything is at true size: ceilings about two and a half metres up, doors two metres tall, a staircase a metre wide, furniture as big as the real thing. So it doesn't feel like a doll's house with you as a giant inside, the rows these houses stand in have been raised a little to give the rooms their proper height. The rooflines still line up.

## Real furniture

The first pass drew the kitchens, stairs and curtains in code, and they looked it. They've been swapped for proper models: kitchen units, a stainless sink and tap, fridges, washing machines, a cooker hood, wooden stairs with banisters, curtains at every front window, and an office chair in the bedroom. Most of them are models other people have shared on Sketchfab under a CC BY licence.

There are no working lights, so the houses are lit by whatever comes through the windows. On a dull day the back rooms get gloomy.

## The crates are upstairs

The crate in each open house has moved up to the first floor, so you'll have to commit to the stairs to get it. The [tactical map](../../map/) says which.

## Taller tents

The army tents by the Osborne Street checkpoint were too low: you could clip your head walking in. They're taller and roomier now, so you can walk through them upright.
