## A street that was fought over

Duncombe Street had its share of litter and wrecked cars, but nothing on it said anyone had fought there. Now it does.

## Bullet strikes and burnt windows

The house fronts facing the streets are marked with bullet strikes: bursts of chipped holes between the windows, round the doors and up between the floors, with the paler brick or block showing where the face has broken away. A few houses took most of it, where the fighting was, and elsewhere there's the odd burst. Some of the boarded-up windows have soot up the wall above them, where the rooms behind burnt out before anyone nailed the boards on.

## Blast marks and rubble

There are blast marks in the roads and a couple on the pavements: the tarmac scooped out and scorched, with bricks, lumps of concrete, glass and grit thrown round them. Bricks have come off some of the garden walls and lie at their feet in a spill of grit. Spent cases lie where people fired from, by the wrecked cars and across the street from the houses that were shot up. And there's more rubbish about: paper, litter, cans and bottles, flattened boxes, tyres, a few toppled chairs and traffic cones.

## Pavements to the walls

The pavements used to stop a few centimetres short of the garden walls, gates and house fronts, and the bare ground showed through in a thin green strip. They run right up to the walls now. The tarmac and kerbstones have depth to them up close, and the weeds grow along the joins at the walls and the kerbs, not up through the middle of the pavement.
