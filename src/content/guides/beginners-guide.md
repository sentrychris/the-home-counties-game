The Home Counties is an extraction shooter. You go into a raid with some gear and try to come out with more. If you die, you lose what you took in.

Your raids start on Duncombe Street in Bletchley: a few streets that were evacuated when the government came to Bletchley Park, and have been picked over by scavs since it moved on.

## The raid loop

1. **Gear up.** In the hideout, choose what to take: a rifle with loaded magazines, armour if you have it, a rig, a backpack for loot, and some meds. Everything you take in is at risk. Buy what you're missing from the [traders](../../traders/), and set up your rifle at the [gunsmith](../gunsmith/).
2. **Deploy.** Press **Start raid** on the Raid tab. You start at one of two points on Water Eaton Road, at the south of the map: the west end by the level crossing, or the east end by the roadworks. The game picks one at random. Each raid also gets its own weather and time of day.
3. **Loot.** Search crates, open houses and dead scavs with <kbd>F</kbd>. Press <kbd>Tab</kbd> to see your gear and how much you're carrying; a weight icon appears by your body figure when you're overloaded.
4. **Extract.** Reach one of the two exits before the raid timer runs out, and stay in the zone for ten seconds. Anything you bring out goes into your stash, and you can sell it to the traders.

A raid on Duncombe Street lasts **18 minutes**. The countdown is in the top-right corner of the screen.

## Getting out

Both exits are the army's old cordon points, abandoned when the soldiers withdrew. They're at the north end of the map, a long way from both starts.

- **Station Footpath**, north-west corner (grid B1). The lane up to Bletchley station.
- **Oliver Road**, north-east corner (grid I1). By the hoarding at the old Sainsbury's.

Each exit is an extraction zone with a **red smoke flare**. If you lose your bearings, look for the smoke above the rooftops; the compass along the top of the screen gives you bearings but doesn't mark the exits. To extract, walk into the zone and stay there for ten seconds. A box at the top of the screen counts you down.

> Work out where the nearest smoke is early in the raid, not when the timer is nearly out.

## What you lose when you die

If you're killed, or the timer reaches zero before you extract, **you lose everything you took into the raid and everything you picked up in it**: weapon, armour, backpack and loot.

The usual advice for extraction shooters applies: **only take in what you can afford to lose.**

## Your first few raids

- **Go in light.** A rifle, a couple of loaded magazines and a bandage. If the raid goes wrong, you haven't lost much.
- **Learn one exit.** Run to it and extract a few times, then try it from the other start.
- **Stay off the road where you can.** The gardens are slower, but you can't be seen from a hundred metres away.
- **Leave time to get out.** Head for an exit with a few minutes still on the clock.
- **Use the map.** The [tactical map](../../map/) shows every crate spot, scav spawn and open house.
- **Read what you find.** Papers left in the houses tell the street's story. Hold <kbd>F</kbd> to take one home and it goes on the board in your hideout.
- **Bring friends.** Up to four can raid together. See [Co-op squads](../co-op-squads/).

## Reading the HUD

The HUD is kept minimal, as in Tarkov and Arena Breakout. Most of the time it's just this:

| Where | What it tells you |
| --- | --- |
| Top | A compass, for bearings only |
| Top right | Time left in the raid |
| Top middle, in an exit | The extract box, counting you out |
| Bottom left, figure | Your body. Hurt parts glow yellow, then orange, then red, and go black when destroyed. A blood drop means that part is bleeding |
| Beside the figure | The gear you're wearing on one side; on the other, bleeds, destroyed limbs, painkillers working and an overload weight |
| Under the figure | Stamina. Sprinting drains it |
| Bottom right | Your ammo, only for a few seconds after <kbd>Alt</kbd>+<kbd>T</kbd>: rounds in the magazine over what you carry of that type, the ammo's name and the fire mode. With a grenade out, the grenade and how many you have |

Getting hit jolts your view and blurs the edge of the screen on the side it came from, which tells you where the shooter is. Losing a lot of blood drains the colour from everything until you patch yourself up.
